About

Background

I hold a PhD in mathematics and have more than a decade of experience across software engineering, data science, machine learning, and computer vision. My work spans both academia and industry, with applications in medical imaging, industrial processes, aviation, construction, and supply chain management.

While much of my earlier work focused on applied machine learning and computer vision, my recent research direction has moved more strongly toward manifold learning, geometry, and the mathematical foundations of deep learning. I am especially interested in how geometric structure can improve our understanding of learned representations, generalization, and data analysis.

Outside research and engineering, I also teach Dominican bachata. Teaching and building dance communities has shaped how I communicate complex material: clearly, patiently, and with strong attention to structure and interaction.

Tools

Open-source and public resources

I aim to build tools that are useful both for research and for practical analysis workflows. The focus is usually on clarity, interpretable structure, and methods that are simple enough to adapt and extend.

GitHub repository

h-NNE

A hierarchical dimensionality reduction method related in spirit to t-SNE and UMAP, but designed for speed, simplicity, and structured coarse-to-fine exploration.

  • Built around hierarchical structure in the data
  • Supports interpretable zoom-like exploration
  • Useful for visualization and data analysis

Dancing

Dominican bachata teaching and community work

Alongside research and engineering, I teach Dominican bachata and help build local dance communities. This part of my work reflects another side of what I value: teaching, communication, rhythm, culture, and creating spaces where people can learn and connect.

I see this as complementary to my technical profile rather than separate from it. Both research and dance teaching involve careful structure, sensitivity to detail, and the ability to guide others through complex patterns.

What I teach
